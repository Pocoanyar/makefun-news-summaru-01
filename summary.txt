World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026.
1. US-brokered Russia-Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi ended after two days with no breakthrough, but agreed a prisoner swap, due within three weeks on Russia's account; envoys Witkoff and Kushner go to Moscow on Thursday (BBC News).
2. Iran said the Strait of Hormuz stays shut until the US meets its seven conditions; Tehran said three tankers hit mines on Tuesday, while Washington says the strait is open. Brent rose to about $92.80 a barrel (Reuters).
3. Nvidia's board met to decide whether to oust CEO Jensen Huang amid a China chip-and-spy scandal: he admits an affair; lawmakers press for export curbs. Its shares fell over 8% after hours (Reuters).
4. The S&P 500 rose 1.3% and the dollar index hit 102.87, its highest since April 2025; futures priced an 87.3% chance of a 25-basis-point Fed rate rise on 9 December (Reuters).
5. At the COP31 summit in Antalya, UN chief Antonio Guterres said the climate crisis is "outrunning us" and urged a fossil-fuel tax; the US stayed away for a second year (Al Jazeera).
6. Israeli attacks in Gaza killed at least seven people, among them goalkeeper Mahmoud Kamal Abdel-Malek, days after playing in a cup for killed athletes (Al Jazeera).
7. The US House voted 211-204 to block the impeachment of Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, accused of waging war on Iran without congressional approval (BBC News).
8. The US charged three alleged Iran-backed operatives over a plot against RAF Fairford, a UK air base used by US forces (BBC News).
9. Kenya discharged its last Ebola patient, starting a 42-day countdown to a disease-free declaration after nine cases (Reuters); in Congo, cases reached 11,042 with 5,387 deaths, its health institute said.
10. China's Wang Yi called his call with Secretary of State Marco Rubio "positive and constructive", a week before presidents Xi and Trump meet at the APEC summit in Shenzhen (Reuters).
11. A bus hit 32 vehicles in Seongnam, South Korea, injuring 57 people (Yonhap).
